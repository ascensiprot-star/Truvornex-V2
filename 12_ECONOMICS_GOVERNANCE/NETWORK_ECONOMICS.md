# Network Economics and Adoption Model

Value comes from lowering coordination cost and uncertainty: find capable participants, access qualified demand, reuse verified business information, and carry workflows through fulfillment and reconciliation.

Network effects are local/category-specific: supply density improves matching; transaction history improves evidence; interoperability reduces onboarding friction; reliable repeat usage improves retention. Unrelated account counts do not create liquidity.

Cold start: select a bounded segment/geography/sector with painful workflow and recruit real buyers and providers together. Secure actual provider capacity and genuine demand before broadening. Measure completed transactions, not sign-ups.

Revenue hypotheses: transparent transaction fees, provider/SME subscriptions, procurement workflow subscriptions, integration fees, optional operational analytics. Validate willingness to pay, unit economics, fairness, regulation, competitive alternatives. Do not monetize sensitive personal data or charge for fundamental privacy, appeal, export, or essential safety access. Fees must be disclosed before agreement.
