# Problem Thesis and Evidence Plan

## Thesis — to be tested

European economic activity is digitally connected but operationally fragmented. Individuals and SMEs may face friction discovering qualified providers, validating counterparties, understanding cross-border requirements, exchanging structured business information, coordinating capacity, and reconciling delivery with payment and documentation. Truvornex may create value by coordinating these steps through an interoperable workflow layer.

This is a hypothesis, not market proof. The existence of a regulation or technology gap does not prove users will adopt Truvornex.

## Evidence signals and sources

The European Commission's Single Market Strategy identifies persistent barriers to cross-border services and business scaling, including regulatory complexity and qualification recognition. Eurostat's Digital Decade reporting documents gaps in SME digital intensity and digital skills. These are research leads, not evidence that one mobile platform is the solution.

- European Commission, Single Market Strategy: https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/single-market/strategy_en
- Eurostat, Digitalisation in Europe 2026: https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/interactive-publications/digitalisation-2026
- European Commission, European Digital Identity: https://commission.europa.eu/topics/digital-economy-and-society/european-digital-identity_en
- ECB, Instant Payments Regulation: https://www.ecb.europa.eu/paym/retail/instant_payments/html/instant_payments_regulation.en.html

## Research method

For every candidate problem, gather (1) direct interviews or observed workflows, (2) process/transaction data lawfully supplied by participants, and (3) official research, standards, or industry evidence. Record country, sector, actor, existing workaround, frequency, cost, risk, and alternatives. Ask participants to reconstruct their last real transaction; do not ask only whether they like the product idea.

## Candidate problem spaces

SME supplier discovery and qualification; cross-border service friction; fragmented availability/capacity; untrustworthy provider claims; inconsistent request/quote/order/fulfillment workflows; structured invoicing and reconciliation; underutilized equipment/facilities/capacity; digital capability gaps among microbusinesses and SMEs.

Do not assume all belong in the first release. Rank by severity, frequency, willingness to adopt/pay, ability to solve, regulatory risk, and network effects.

## Required evidence output

For every selected problem, keep a statement, affected actor, workflow map, evidence log, alternatives analysis, willingness-to-pay evidence, solution hypothesis, measurable success criteria, and disconfirming evidence. A problem is not validated because it sounds important.
