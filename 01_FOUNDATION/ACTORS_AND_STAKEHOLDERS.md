# Actors and Stakeholders

## Primary actors

- **Individual:** discovers services, requests work, accepts agreements, tracks delivery, uses partner-managed payment, and manages evidence/preferences.
- **Independent professional:** represents skills/qualifications, publishes services and availability, quotes work, fulfills commitments, receives partner-managed payouts.
- **Service provider:** manages catalog, staff, locations, capacity, schedules, quotes, orders, and customer communication.
- **Business buyer:** creates procurement requests, evaluates suppliers, manages approvals, accepts delivery, reconciles invoices.
- **Organization administrator:** manages legal-entity references, members, roles, policy, billing, and audit access.
- **Institutional integrator:** accesses expressly authorized workflows/data through documented agreements and APIs.
- **Platform operations:** supports users, reviews fraud/safety escalations, administers disputes under strict access controls.
- **Regulated partner:** identity provider, PSP, e-invoice provider, credential issuer, or other authorized institution.

## Account is not actor

Person, login account, organization, legal entity, business location, and delegated representative are distinct. One person may act personally and for multiple organizations. Login does not imply legal authority to bind every organization linked to the person.

## Authority model

Use scoped, revocable delegation. A member may manage listings but not approve payments or execute contracts. Authorize against organization, resource, action, amount, jurisdiction, and workflow state—not role label alone.

## Stakeholder protections

Explain who controls each record, who can view/change it, and who is accountable if a claim is wrong. Operations staff must not have broad access to private messages, identity evidence, or business documents by default. Model impersonation, coercion, collusion, harassment, discriminatory exclusion, retaliation, and review manipulation.
