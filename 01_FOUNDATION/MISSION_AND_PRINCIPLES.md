# Mission, System Intent, and Principles

## Mission

Truvornex coordinates economic activity between people and organizations by making needs, capabilities, resources, commitments, delivery, and economic outcomes discoverable, verifiable, and interoperable across supported markets.

## System intent

Reduce friction and uncertainty in finding a capable counterparty, establishing fair terms, coordinating delivery, proving completion, and reconciling economic exchange. Help small actors participate in digital economic networks without requiring enterprise-scale software.

## What it is

- A coordination layer for demand, supply, capacity, and economic workflows.
- A trust/evidence layer that explains why a claim is considered verified.
- A transaction workflow layer for offers, commitments, fulfillment, disputes, and payment references.
- An interoperability layer for authorized identity, business, payment, invoicing, and service systems.
- An intelligence layer for permitted operational insights and decision support.

## What it is not

A government, bank, payment institution, identity issuer, credit bureau, insurer, employer, court, tax authority, or replacement for public registries. It is not a broad social network optimized for engagement, nor a generic directory with no verified availability or completion evidence.

## Design principles

1. Evidence over assertion: claims need provenance, scope, freshness, and verification method.
2. User agency: users understand data disclosure and meaningful consequences.
3. Least privilege: actors receive only capabilities required for the task.
4. Explicit commitments: proposals, quotes, acceptances, cancellations, and amendments are distinct records.
5. No invisible money: every amount has currency, payer/payee, purpose, status, and reconciliation reference.
6. Interoperability: use established standards and public infrastructure where available.
7. Jurisdictional honesty: support is declared per country and capability.
8. Fail-safe degradation: AI, maps, chat, analytics, and individual providers cannot corrupt core transactions.
9. Accessibility and localization are foundational.
10. Modular simplicity: preserve domain boundaries without premature distributed complexity.
11. No coercive lock-in: portability, exit, transparent fees, and appeal are trust requirements.
12. Measure successful economic utility, not screen time.
